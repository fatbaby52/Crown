Responding to a DBE Reevaluation RFI

If your certifying agency has asked for additional information on your DBE reevaluation packet, treat it as the most important filing of the process. If the reevaluation ends in decertification, the USDOT appeal under 49 CFR § 26.89 is decided on the existing record—no new evidence. Your RFI response is your last chance to build that record.

Crown Consulting prepares complete, deadline-compliant RFI responses for California DBE firms in reevaluation review.

Why RFIs Are Going Out Now

Caltrans and the other CUCP certifying agencies are working through the reevaluation packets submitted by the April 16, 2026 deadline. Caltrans has told on-time filers to expect status updates and to take no action "unless contacted by your certifying agency." When you are contacted, it's usually one of three things: a request for additional information, a notice of decision, or a scheduling communication. This page covers the first—and the RFI is the one where what you do next changes the outcome.

An RFI Is a Good Sign—If You Answer It Well

Reviewers issue RFIs when the packet doesn't let them confirm eligibility, but doesn't compel denial either. The reviewer is telling you exactly where the record is thin. A complete, organized, on-time response resolves the gap; a partial response converts a fixable problem into a denial.

Common RFI Triggers We See

Personal Net Worth documentation. Caltrans' own guidance flags the recurring problems:

  • Incomplete account statements—every page of every document is required
  • Screenshots submitted in place of full statements (not accepted)
  • Statement dates that don't match the "as of" date on the PNW form, or balances that don't tie
  • Household property valued without a stated valuation method—or confused with the home's value
  • Math errors and unsigned or undated forms

Personal Narrative gaps. Claims without dates, places, or outcomes; no documentary support for a material incident; no clear link between the disadvantage described and a business or career impact; inconsistencies between the narrative and the tax returns, resume, or ownership records elsewhere in the packet.

Ownership and control questions. Operating agreements that don't reflect claimed ownership percentages, licenses held by a non-disadvantaged owner, or signature authority that undercuts the control story.

How to Respond

  • Answer every item, in order, completely. Map each request to a labeled exhibit. Don't make the reviewer hunt.
  • Fix the underlying inconsistency, not just the symptom. If the PNW doesn't tie to the bank statements, reconcile it and explain the reconciliation.
  • Add narrative context where the evidence needs it. A short cover memo that walks the reviewer through the response is worth more than a stack of unexplained PDFs.
  • Meet the deadline. If you genuinely can't, request an extension in writing before the deadline passes—not after.
  • Think about the appeal you hope never to file. Everything you want a USDOT reviewer to see later must be in the record now.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did I get a request for additional information on my DBE reevaluation?

Certifying agencies are now reviewing the reevaluation packets submitted by April 16, 2026. When a reviewer can't confirm eligibility from the packet alone—an unsupported Personal Narrative claim, an incomplete Personal Net Worth entry, a missing account statement—they issue an RFI rather than deciding immediately. An RFI means the reviewer sees a gap you can still fill.

How important is the RFI response?

Critical. If your firm is decertified after reevaluation, the appeal to USDOT under 49 CFR 26.89 is decided on the record as it existed when the certifier made its decision—no new evidence is accepted. Your RFI response is effectively your last opportunity to put evidence in the record.

What are the most common documentation problems in DBE reevaluation packets?

Caltrans flags recurring PNW issues: incomplete account statements (all pages of every document are required), screenshots instead of full statements, statement dates that don't match the PNW form's as-of date, missing valuation methods for household property, math errors, and unsigned forms. On the narrative side: claims without dates, documents, or a link to business impact.

What happens if I miss the RFI deadline or respond incompletely?

The certifier decides on the record it has. An incomplete or late response typically leads to denial of continued eligibility, and the gaps can't be cured on appeal. If you can't meet the deadline, ask your certifying agency for an extension in writing before it passes.
