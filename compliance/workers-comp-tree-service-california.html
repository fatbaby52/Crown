Workers' Comp for Tree Service Contractors in California

For a California tree service, workers' compensation is often the largest cost on the books after payroll itself—and the easiest one to overpay. Tree work is among the most dangerous occupations in the country, and that risk is priced directly into your premium. But a large share of what tree service contractors pay comes down to classification, claims history, and audits—not the work itself.

Crown Consulting helps tree care and vegetation management contractors get their workers' comp right: paying for the risk they actually carry, not the risk a misclassification says they carry.

Why Tree Service Workers' Comp Is So Expensive

California workers' comp premiums are built on classification codes set by the WCIRB, and tree work sits near the top of the rate table. The classification that drives most tree service premiums is 0106, Tree Pruning, Repairing or Trimming—any operation where part of the work requires elevation, whether by climbing, ladders, or aerial lifts. Because of the fall and struck-by exposure, code 0106 is one of the higher-rated classifications in the state, commonly running several times the rate of general landscaping.

There is a second classification that matters: 0042, Landscape Gardening, which applies to employees working exclusively on the ground—crews who only run the chipper, drag brush, or drive. The rate for 0042 is dramatically lower than 0106. If your ground-only payroll is being swept into the 0106 rate, you are very likely overpaying.

It's also worth knowing that the CSLB requires holders of the D-49 Tree Service classification to carry workers' compensation insurance regardless of whether they have employees—tree service is held to a stricter standard than most trades because of its risk profile.

Getting Your Classification Right

The single most common—and most expensive—mistake we see is payroll that should be split between 0106 and 0042 being reported entirely under the high-rate 0106 code. California rules allow legitimate division of payroll when ground-only employees are genuinely separate and the records support it. That requires accurate job descriptions, time records, and payroll documentation that an auditor will accept.

This is not about gaming the system. It's about reporting your operation accurately so you pay the rate that matches the actual exposure of each crew. Done correctly, a proper classification review can change your premium materially without changing a single thing about how you run jobs.

Lowering Your Experience Modification Rate (EMR)

Your experience modification rate is the multiplier applied to your base premium based on your claims history versus other employers in your class. An EMR below 1.0 lowers your premium; above 1.0 raises it. For a tree service, even a modest EMR improvement can mean tens of thousands of dollars a year, and a high EMR can disqualify you from bidding work that requires a 1.0 or lower.

EMR is driven by the frequency and cost of claims over a three-year window. The levers that actually move it are fewer and less severe claims, a documented return-to-work program that keeps injured employees productive on light duty, and aggressive, well-managed claims handling. We help contractors build the programs and documentation that bring an EMR down and keep it there.

Surviving the Annual Premium Audit

Every workers' comp policy is audited at the end of the term, and for tree services the audit can swing your final premium by a large margin. Auditors reclassify payroll, reassign uninsured subcontractors to your policy, and add back amounts you can't document. The contractors who get hurt at audit are the ones without clean records.

We help you prepare so the audit confirms what you reported instead of inflating it: organized payroll by classification, certificates of insurance for every subcontractor, clear records separating ground and aerial work, and documentation for any owner or clerical exclusions.

Safety Programs That Reduce Risk—and Premium

The durable way to lower workers' comp cost is to have fewer injuries, and that comes from a real safety program. Tree care operations in California are governed by the ANSI Z133 safety standard for arboricultural operations and by Cal/OSHA's requirements, including a written Injury and Illness Prevention Program. A program that exists only on paper won't protect your crews or your EMR.

We build safety and compliance programs your crews can actually follow in the field and your supervisors can document—the foundation that drives both fewer claims and a better experience modification rate.
